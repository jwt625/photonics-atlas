2. WHAT THE PAPER FIXES
-----------------------
Fixed (basis stated, all Colladon, Comptes rendus 1842):
- Purpose (p.800): make the shapes of a fluid vein leaving various orifices visible to all students; light the vein
  from inside, in a dark space; "une des plus belles et des plus curieuses expériences" of an optics course.
- Vessel (p.800): "vase parallélipédique de 1 mètre de hauteur". THE ONLY NUMBER IN THE LETTER (dates aside).
- Orifice (p.800): on one face, "un peu au-dessus du fond", an opening taking screw-in diaphragms of different sizes.
- Jet (p.800): leaves horizontally; title: parabolic vein.
- Lens (p.800): hole in the opposite wall "sur la même direction", convex lens fitted to it.
- Tube (p.800): outside the vessel, horizontal, blackened inside, keeps rays oblique to the jet axis out.
- Room (p.800): darkened room; a shutter pierced with a hole takes the blackened tube.
- Light (p.800): sunlight sent by a mirror parallel to the tube axis.
- Path (p.800): rays cross lens and liquid, converge in the orifice; inside the vein they meet the surface at a small
  enough angle for total internal reflection, again at each new incidence; light circulates "comme dans un canal" and
  follows all the bends.
- Visibility (p.800-801): clear water + clean diaphragm edge: vein barely visible though intense light circulates.
- Escape (p.801): where the vein meets a solid, the light escapes and contact points become luminous; a basin set
  horizontally gets its bottom lit.
- Drops (p.801): from a great height or with a diameter of only a few mm, the vein breaks into drops in its lower
  part; only there does the liquid light up; each rupture point throws out a bright light.
- Savart vibrating-plate experiments repeatable (p.801); any jet direction can be lit with reflectors (p.801).
- Precaution (p.801): water at room temperature, so no dew forms on the outer face of the lens.
- Clouded water (p.801): to see the jet near the orifice (contraction of the vein), cloud the water with solutions or
  dust; light disperses on leaving the vessel and the vein glows in its upper part.
- Fissures (p.801): small taps on the vessel near the orifice break the vein in the orifice plane; bright fissures,
  sometimes persisting down the vein.
- Dating (p.801-802): see spec title_note. No "first" claim in the letter.
Assumed (rendering only, each flagged in the spec): vessel inside 300 x 300 mm, wall 10 mm, orifice centre 100 mm above
  the inner floor, water level 900 mm (head 800 mm, held constant), apertures 10 mm (continuous) and 3 mm (drops
  chapter, stands for "quelques millimètres"), diaphragm plate 60 mm, lens 80 mm, tube 300 mm x 80 mm bore, shutter
  panel 1210 x 900 mm, mirror 200 x 200 mm at 270 mm outside the shutter, sun elevation 30 deg from +z, stand 500 mm,
  basin 500 mm x 120 mm (water 90 mm), drop break-up from mid-path.
Derived: jet path y = x^2/(4h) (Torricelli efflux, horizontal launch, contraction and friction ignored), reach
  1290 mm for h = 800 mm and 520 mm drop; lens focal distance in water 315 mm (focus at the orifice); mirror normal
  (0.707, 0.354, 0.612) = bisector of +x and the reversed assumed sun direction (check: reflected ray = (1, 0, 0)).
Figure basis (LN 1884 engraving, no scale): open-top box; vessel raised on a pedestal on a table; basin on a stool.
Withheld: 13 entries in the spec (sizes, level upkeep, apertures, jet numbers, lens data, tube size, mirror / sun,

5. CHAPTER SCRIPT RATIONALE
---------------------------
Order follows the letter: apparatus (vessel, diaphragm, jet, lens and tube), then the darkened room and sunlight, then
the optics (convergence in the orifice, total reflection along the jet), then the three observations the letter
reports: light escaping into the basin with the clear jet barely visible, drops lighting up (thin jet), and clouded
water making the upper jet glow. Physics checks for the builder:
- the room goes dark before sunlight enters; the beam is parallel outside, converges inside the water to the orifice;
- in clear water the jet must stay faint while the basin bottom lights (the letter's main observation); the glowing
  jet only appears in the clouded-water chapter, brightest at the orifice and fading downstream;
- drops: only the broken lower part lights, each rupture flashes; upper continuous part dark;
- rays in the jet are schematic, at glancing incidence, reflecting at each incidence and following the curve;
- the jet path does not change with lighting; level held constant (assumed).
Essential on-canvas dims: only the vessel height (1 m) is sourced; the story has 1 essential dim instead of 2-4
(see open questions). One amber emphasis: "*Total reflection*". No numbers in lines except "one metre".
Light source: sunlight per the letter (DevLog idea mentioned the arc lamp; the lamp is an 1884 addition, kept to the
drawing pane, the beam part detail and withheld).
