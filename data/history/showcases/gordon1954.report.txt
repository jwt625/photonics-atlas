2. WHAT THE PAPER FIXES (fixed / assumed / withheld per part)
-------------------------------------------------------------
Letter locators: p.282 col. 2 (abstract-like opening, source, focuser), p.283 col. 1 (focuser forces, spectrometer
operation, oscillation, amplifier, cavity, velocity), p.283 col. 2 (4 kc/sec expected, 6-8 kc/sec observed, hyperfine
structure), Figs. 1-3 on p.283.
- NH3 source: fixed = exists, on the axis, end with many holes (Fig. 1 END X-SECTION; patent FIG. 5 apertures 45);
  source pressure about 10^-3 mm Hg (patent col. 4:48-49). Assumed = tube OD 16 mm, wall 1.5, 60 mm shown. Withheld =
  hole count / size (FIG. 5 pattern used as drawn, 33 holes, schematic; letter's end view draws fewer).
- Focuser: fixed = quadrupolar cylindrical electrostatic field along the beam (letter p.282-283); four electrodes, V and
  O on alternate diagonals (letter Fig. 1, patent FIG. 6; FIG. 6 wires V to the source, O to ground); upper states
  pushed in, lower out (letter p.283; patent col. 5:14-23 with the field proportional to radius). Patent example values
  (col. 4:56-59): about 2 cm diameter, about 20 cm length, minimum spacing about 4 mm, 20-30 kV. Profile: digitized
  from the letter's Fig. 1 cross-section (scan px, crop origin (1162, 1529), mean electrode width 128 px) and scaled
  to 20 mm width: neighbour gaps 3.1 / 3.3 / 4.2 / 4.5 mm (consistent with the patent's "about 4 mm"), clear radius at
  the axis 4.8-5.7 mm. Patent FIG. 6 has much wider gaps (gap / width 0.63, clear radius / width 0.54) and is not used
  for proportions. Liquid-air cooling = patent option only (withheld for 1954).
- Cavity: fixed = cylindrical, copper, TE011, 12 cm long, coaxial with the beam, field sin(pi x / L) (letter p.283
  col. 1); diameter about 0.6 in, tuned by a sliding end section, entrance aperture 413 about 0.4 in (patent col.
  5:27-32). Derived check: TE011 resonance f = (c / 2 pi) sqrt((3.8317 / a)^2 + (pi / L)^2) at 23 870 Mc/sec with
  L = 120 mm needs a = 7.670 mm, D = 15.34 mm (0.604 in): consistent with "about 0.6 inch" (with L = 127 mm: 15.34 mm).
  Assumed = wall 1.5 mm. Withheld = end construction, exit hole.
- Guides: fixed = output guide on the top near the upstream end, input guide at the downstream end (letter Fig. 1,
  patent FIG. 4; measured positions 0.22 (letter) and 0.27 (patent) of the cavity length for the output guide; the input
  guide runs down along the downstream end wall in FIG. 4); mica windows (patent col. 5:44-46). Assumed = 10 x 5 mm
  rectangular stand-ins. Withheld = guide size and shape, coupling holes.
- Chamber 417 / pump connection 418: patent only (col. 4:50-52, FIG. 4), 10^-4 mm Hg; shape and size assumed (faint box,
  30 mm stub).
- Leads 426 / 427: patent FIG. 4 / FIG. 6 only; plain wires.
- Beam: velocity 4 x 10^4 cm/sec (stated); cavity transit 0.3 ms (derived, L/v); "virtually all in the upper states"
  at the cavity (stated). Particle counts, spread, and the half-and-half mix at the source are illustrative.

5. CHAPTER SCRIPT RATIONALE
---------------------------
Order follows the beam and the letter: source -> focuser built -> voltage on, states separate (levels plot) -> cavity
-> emission in the cavity with klystron power in (spectrometer) -> klystron sweep with the Fig. 3b plot (drive offset
= plot pen x, glow / output brightness from the digitized curve, D1006 rule) -> input off, oscillation held. Physics
checks for the builder: no focusing before the voltage is on; lower-state molecules leave inside the focuser and never
reach the cavity; molecules change colour only inside the cavity, after entering through the aperture; the field glow
follows sin(pi x / L) along the axis, zero at both ends; output leaves through the output guide (top, upstream end),
input enters through the input guide (downstream end); in the oscillation chapter the input fades while the output
stays; no 23 870 Mc/sec label on the oscillation. Beam motion is slowed (hover says not to scale).
Amber emphasis: the spectrum keyword "*23 870 Mc/sec*" (the one essential frequency). Plot chapters: 2 (levels,
spectrum); Fig. 3a, 3c, 3d are alternates of the spectrum plot.
